1 OpeningJustus, 25 s

Think of your last workout. You finish a set, out of breath, and you'd have to type: ten reps, fifty kilos. Most people don't. So we built this: you lift, we count. A small sensor on the strength machines a gym already owns counts sets and reps by itself. Members see them without typing, and the owner sees how each machine is used.

2 TeamJustus, 20 s

We are four co-founders with four clear roles. I do sales and the owner app. Adam leads product development, the sensor and the iPhone app; he and Brijan built the first unit together. Bennett leads our research: the interviews and the Dutch market. Brijan does the hardware design, the housing. Adam, over to you.

3 ProblemAdam, 35 s

Gym owners decide by feel which machines to buy, move, service or retire. German gyms invested about 83,000 euros each last year, and about 80 percent plan to invest this year, yet their software only counts who walks in. And most members log nothing: fitness apps keep 3 percent of their users after 30 days, and none of our first five interviewees keeps a log.

4 Value propositionAdam, 35 s

So the machine does the counting. The owner pays and sees each machine's use, with a weekly report on what changed, so machines are bought, moved, serviced or retired by their use. Members get their log free: sets and reps without typing, the weight in one tap. The owner app is still a prototype on sample data. Of ten ideas in our COCD box, this one survived once the gym, an organisation with a budget, became the payer.

5 PrototypesAdam, 40 s

The counting works on real machines: you lift, the ring fills, the set is saved. We ran it for five days in a gym, 18 to 27 September, on more than ten machine types; on the first day it counted 45 sets on six machines. Its limits: one lifter, from our team, and setting up a new machine failed twice, fixed within days. We show no accuracy figure until our own pass mark is met. Next, we run real sets at FitPods Brno. Bennett, what did people tell us?

6 Test matrixBennett, 45 s

We tested twice. In test 1, eight people: three rated a log without typing well before we described our idea, but none of the first five keeps a log, so we sell to the gym owner. In test 2, three Dutch gyms and two members: nothing clean yet. One manager would look at a low-risk pilot, said right after hearing our idea, and no gym felt a strong need. So we narrowed our target and added a free trial. We reached 8 and 5 of the 10 people the course asks for; the eight owner conversations that decide test 2 are still to come.

7 CustomersBennett, 30 s

We start with independent gyms with ten or more machines: about 724 within a day trip of Baden-Württemberg, and about 755 in the Netherlands. Our whole market, the TAM: 40,965 such gyms in Europe, 393 million euros a year at our price. The SAM, the five countries around us: 9,511 gyms, 91 million a year. Our SOM: 264 gyms by January 2030 in the planning case, 2.8 percent of that, about 2.5 million euros a year. All estimates; chains come later.

8 CompetitorsBennett, 35 s

Our corner is not empty. Smart machines count by themselves, but only on their makers' own machines. Member trackers like Trackbar give members a screen, not owners a view. Usage-data vendors such as Cyclops already show owners how machines are used, though we found none in Germany or the Netherlands. We add the members' own log, with no screen, on any brand's strength machines. Cyclops is the closest, and the one to watch. Brijan, how do we make money?

9 Business Model CanvasBrijan, 35 s

The gym pays per machine: 249 euros once and 20 euros a month, for at least ten machines and twelve months; members use it free. We start with independent gyms that have a machine decision coming. Today our partner is a lawyer; dealers and service partners come only after ten paying gyms. Our main costs are the software and hardware, sensors under 50 euros each with a target of about 40, and the free trials and sales trips.

point at each field

The Brand Key is our target position until the first trials show it. Its essence, every set seen, promises a record, not a result. For the owner, the insight is: my software shows me the door, not the floor. 11 Marketing mixBrijan, 35 s

From it come our 4Ps. Product: sensors on the gym's own strength machines, any brand, plus an owner app and a member app. Price: 249 euros per machine once, then 20 euros per machine a month. Place: walk-ins near Baden-Württemberg, first trials in the Netherlands. Promotion: a free four-week trial. Our target: at least seven orders from the first twenty trials by June 2027; if fewer order, we plan with the published 25 percent.

12 Scenario matrixBennett, 30 s

The market drifts toward chains with smart machines, but machines stay on gym floors for years. So we start where ordinary machines stay, with independent gyms, and add chains later. If chains buy only smart machines, our move is to cover the rest of the floor, or to work with the machine makers. That is our reading for the next two to three years, not a forecast. Hard questions, honest answers

What is the 130,000 euros for?

It pays for the free trials and the certification until the first gym pays us, in May 2027, plus a reserve. Until then we spend 102,336 euros: 38,200 for our trial sensors, 21,983 for paid helpers at the pilots and first trials, 20,955 for certification (safety tests, registrations, an outside code review, an industry fee), 8,912 for company and legal, and 12,286 for running costs, sales, launch, tools and interest. A reserve of 27,381 brings it to 129,717. We put in 19,114 ourselves; 115,000 comes from outside in two parts, 85,000 when we found the company in February 2027 and 30,000 after the proof of concept in May. That money is planned, not secured, and its source is still open.

How sure are the roadmap and the finances?

Not sure, and the plan says so itself: every conversion rate is untested, and it is a plan with dated gates, not a result. Only two dates are planned, the first pilot and the founding; every later month comes from our planning case. August 2028 is not a forecast but a check: do at least 18 of the first 20 gyms renew? If not, we plan with the published 80.6 percent. We don't know August 2028; the gates tell us early when we are wrong, and every miss moves us to the conservative case.

Do you stand behind the Brand Key and the canvas?

The Brand Key is our target position until the first trials show it, and every field ends with what we still have to prove. In the canvas, the 40-euro sensor is a target. It is our target position, not a finished brand.

How accurate is the counting?

We publish no accuracy figure yet, and our plan says so: not until our own pass mark of 40 checked sets is read. What we can say: five days in a gym, 18 to 27 September, on more than ten machine types, with one lifter from our team. Next come real sets at FitPods Brno.

Why would a gym pay, if you found no strong need?

That is our biggest open question, and why we start with owners who have a machine decision coming, and with a free trial. Our test: seven orders from the first twenty trials. If 60 visits bring two orders or fewer, we stop extra sales spending.

Isn't the price higher than the value?

In our planning case, yes: a gym can count about 0.64 euros of value for every euro it pays, and that is an estimate, not a measurement. For an owner with a machine decision coming, the value is higher. We promise no savings, and we show the value every quarter.

Why not sell the sensor to members?

In test 1, members turned down a product for members only, and logging apps keep only 3 percent of users. The gym owns the machines, so the gym pays, and members use it for free.

What about charging and broken sensors?

The sensors must be charged; how long the battery lasts on a busy machine is still being measured. During the trial we pay for any damage our sensors cause.

Who stops a big maker from copying you?

Nothing technical, and we don't claim it. Makers show use only on their own machines; we work on any brand, which a maker has little reason to do.

How much does a sensor cost to make?

Our target is about 40 euros each with assembly, and under 50. Production is bought in, not done by us.

What if you don't find the 115,000 euros?

Then we don't found the company. The first part of the money must be promised before we do.
