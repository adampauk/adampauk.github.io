1 OpeningJustus, 25 s

Think of your last workout. You finish a set, you're out of breath, and you open an app to type: ten reps, fifty kilos. Then again after the next set. Most people give up on that. So we built this: you lift, we count. A small sensor on the machines a gym already has records every set and every rep by itself.

2 TeamJustus, 20 s

We are four co-founders. I study management and sales in Heilbronn and spent a year in sales at a Stuttgart start-up. Adam studies entrepreneurship in Brno; he founded an IT company and a gym. Bennett studies human resource management at Windesheim, and Brijan is in his final year of mechanical engineering there. Adam, over to you.

3 ProblemAdam, 40 s

Writing down your training interrupts it, so most people stop. Health and fitness apps keep only 3 percent of their users after 30 days. In our interviews, none of the first five people keeps a training log. The gym owner has the same blind spot from the other side: German gyms invested 83,000 euros each last year, but their software only counts who walks in. Machines are bought, moved and retired by feel.

4 Value propositionAdam, 35 s

So the machine does the counting. Members get every set and rep recorded with nothing to type, the weight in one tap and the rest timed by itself, for free. The gym owner pays, and sees how often each machine is used, every week, so decisions are based on use, not feel. We picked this from ten ideas in our COCD box: it won once we learned that the gym, not the member, would pay.

5 PrototypesAdam, 45 s

And it works today. You lift, the ring fills with each rep, and the set is saved. Over the last two weeks I checked 35 sets in the app, and 34 were counted exactly; one was off by one rep. That was on 14 different machines and exercises. To be fair about it: I am the only lifter so far, I checked my own sets, and five times it saw a set that wasn't one. Next we test other lifters and seat settings. Bennett, what did people tell us?

6 Test matrixBennett, 45 s

We tested twice. In test 1 we talked to eight people. Three rated a log without typing well before we even described our idea, but none of the first five keeps a log today, so we decided to sell to the gym owner. In test 2 we talked to three Dutch gyms and two members. One gym saw value on all its ordinary machines, but no gym felt a strong need yet, and one guessed a much lower price than ours. So we narrowed our target and added a free four-week trial. We have interview findings, but no gym has paid yet.

7 CustomersBennett, 30 s

So who exactly are our first customers? Independent gyms with ten or more machines. We will visit 1,479 of them first: 724 near Baden-Württemberg and 755 in the Netherlands. With our standard 40 sensors, each gym pays 9,600 euros a year in fees. If all of them signed, that would be 14.2 million euros a year. Chains come later.

8 CompetitorsBennett, 40 s

What do these gyms and members use today? Paper, notes apps or logging apps, which all mean typing. Smart machines from EGYM, Technogym or milon count by themselves, but only on their own machines, so a gym would have to buy new ones. Add-on trackers like Trackbar need a screen on each machine. We work on the machines the gym already has. Our trade-off: sensors must be fitted and charged, and so far only one lifter has checked the counting. Brijan, how do we make money?

9 Business Model CanvasBrijan, 40 s

Here is the business model in one canvas. The gym pays per machine: 249 euros once and 20 euros a month, for at least ten machines and twelve months. Members use it for free. Our key activities are fitting the sensors, running free trials, building both apps and selling. Our main costs are building the software and hardware, the sensors at about 40 euros each, and the free trials and sales trips.

Hard questions, honest answers

How accurate is the counting?

From 4 to 12 October, 34 of the 35 sets I checked were exact, on 14 machines and exercises; one was off by one rep, and five times it saw a set that wasn't one. That is one lifter checking his own sets, so it is a first sign, not proof. Next: other lifters and seat settings.

Why would a gym pay, if you found no strong need?

That is our biggest open question, and why we start with owners who have a machine decision coming, and with a free trial. Our test: seven orders from the first twenty trials. If 60 visits bring two orders or fewer, we stop extra sales spending.

Isn't the price higher than the value?

Today, yes: a gym can count about 0.64 euros of value for every euro it pays, and we say so. For an owner with a machine decision coming, the value is higher. We show the value every quarter, and we promise no savings.

Why not sell the sensor to members?

In test 1, members turned down a product for members only, and logging apps keep only 3 percent of users. The gym owns the machines, so the gym pays, and members use it for free.

What about charging and broken sensors?

The sensors must be charged; how long the battery lasts on a busy machine is still being measured. During the trial we pay for any damage our sensors cause.

Who stops a big maker from copying you?

Nothing technical, and we don't claim it. Makers show use only on their own machines; we work on any brand, which a maker has little reason to do.

How much does a sensor cost to make?

Our target is about 40 euros each with assembly, and under 50. Production is bought in, not done by us.

What if you don't find the 115,000 euros?

Then we don't found the company. The first part of the money must be promised before we do.
