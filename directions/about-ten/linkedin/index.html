01the first post

Adam PaukFounder, about ten.  now

I run FitPods, a small private gym in Brno. For two years I watched the same thing: people lose count of their reps around six, round up to ten, and a month later wonder why nothing changes.

So we built a small box that sits on the weight stack of a machine and counts every rep, every kilo and every rest. Members tap in with their phone, lift, and see the real numbers after. No typing, no watch.

We call it about ten. Because that's what everyone answers when you ask how many they did.

We're looking for three gyms for a four-week pilot on up to ten machines. If that's you, my messages are open.

Most members who quit don't quit because of price. They quit because they can't see that anything is working.

They lose count at six, the mirror shows no change, and somewhere in week three or four they stop coming.

What changes that is a real number. When the box shows a member that their leg press went from 70 to 85 kg in a month, they come back to beat it.

In our sample gym, 12 of 13 new members were still training in week four. [sample numbers, swap before posting]

Happy to show any gym owner what that looks like on their own floor.

04the referral pilot
AP
Adam PaukFounder, about ten.  now

A different way to sell to gyms: we don't call the owner first.

We let members ask for it. Every gym gets a page with ten dots. Each member who signs up fills one and gets a black and white portrait, 'rat no. 7'. When a gym reaches ten, we call the owner with a free pilot.

An owner who hears 'ten of your members asked for this' listens differently than one who hears a cold pitch.

FitPods filled its ten first. Which Brno gym is next?
