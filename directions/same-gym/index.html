same gym

Lift together.

Gyms buy it because members stay. A sensor on every machine counts every rep, set and rest. Members see their own progress and beat their own bests first. Then the gym joins in: spots, one shared number each week, challenges, gym against gym.

Open the websiteTap through the app

The idea in one chain

Counting makes progress visible. Visible progress is worth cheering. Cheered members stay.

It countsA sensor on every weight-stack machine counts every rep, set and rest. Members tap in once and lift.
You see itEvery member sees their own progress: new bests, weights going up, when to go heavier, streaks.
The gym sees itWorkouts land in the gym's feed. Members spot each other, the week has one shared total, challenges run for every level.
Members stayPeople stay where they get stronger and where someone notices. That is what the gym pays for.

Community is stressed more here than in the direction for lifters, but the whole product is told: counting, progress and insights first, the social side on top. Most challenges are against your own last month. Members against members, and gym against gym, come second.

Motion

  • Numbers count up once, when first seen.
  • App screens slide like iOS, tabs switch at once.
  • A spot gives a short press and pop.
  • The gym screen breathes: a new best every few seconds.
  • No confetti, no mascot, nothing bouncing.

What changed from version 3

Properly this time.

  • Only orange. The navy is gone, white and ink carry the rest.
  • Many colour photos of people, from the pilot gym's shoot, with the high five as the hero.
  • The whole product is told: reps, sets, rest, the speed of each rep, weights and seat settings kept, when to go heavier, progress over years.
  • Your own best comes first. New bests, streaks, milestones and most improved, then the gym, then gym against gym.
  • The website talks to gyms: retention, the first 30 days, members who go quiet, trainers, proof measured in a pilot.
  • Everything is built: the app to tap through, the gym screen, the owner view, three e-mails, six posts, three stories and the print.
  • Harry sits this one out. No mascot, nothing childish. He can come back for milestones if you want him.
  • Grounded in the fieldwork: what 35 gym contacts said in October shapes the owner side, including what speaks against it.
