Brand directions, 19 Nov 2026

One sensor, nine stories.

The product stays the same: a sensor on each gym machine counts the reps, times the rest and keeps the phone in the bag. The story behind it can sit anywhere between a gym joke and a lab, and between the number and the rest of your life. Each direction below has its own idea, and each one changes what the app has to be best at.

Where we are

Round three: four brands, each one built whole. same gym, easy does it, ziitra and lift for later, rebuilt after your notes, each with its own name, a product website, an app drawn from it, posts in phone mock-ups and e-mails.

Dropped: the data is the joke. Folded in: wake up your machines, as the pitch to gyms inside each brand, and nothing to log, as the line “nothing to type between sets” in every brand.

Round two: copy and ours, side by side. Each look became a design skill, and the skill made our version of every piece.

Ordered by score. Each card has a postcard in that direction's own type and colour, and the brands it can learn from.

The data is the joke

87of 100

To copy: Spotify, Spotify for Artists and the Spotify app

Real numbers from real gyms are funnier than anything we could write.

Gyms are full of strange, very human habits that nobody has ever counted. The sensor counts all of them, so the brand never has to invent a joke or a claim. The website stays as calm and exact as readout, and the ads simply publish the numbers.

Who it is for
Members who share things, and gyms that need content for their own socials and TV.
The way in
A weekly gym fact from the first gym’s real numbers, on its screens and socials. Billboards in the city with gym-wide numbers.
The look
Spotify’s look: black pages, one bright green, big bold titles, white pill buttons, real people in the photos. Loud ads with a number first.
What the app must do
Made like the Spotify app: shelves of your machines and plans, and the live set in the mini player. Exact before anything else, because every joke rests on a correct number.
Why it could win
It solves the serious brand nobody shares without turning into a joke brand. Every laugh is proof that the counting works.
The risk
A joke about one person feels creepy, Netflix learned that in 2017. Only gym-wide numbers, never a single member, and only with the gym's yes.

Reference brands

  • SpotifyIts 2016 billboards turned listening data into jokes in 14 markets, and Wrapped made data a yearly ritual.
  • NetflixOne data joke in 2017 got over 400,000 likes and a privacy backlash. It shows both the reach and the line not to cross.
  • StravaThe yearly Year in Sport report turns users' numbers into a news story every December.
  • DuolingoA serious learning product with a completely unserious voice on social.

The gym is the last place the future hasn't reached. We're bringing it, for everyone.

Phones, cars, homes and watches all got smart. The gym still runs on guesswork, paper and memory. A small sensor on every machine turns any gym into a smart gym, made so simple that everyone understands it, from the first-timer to the owner.

Don't replace your machines. Wake them up.

Every gym is full of machines that work perfectly and know nothing. New smart machines cost a fortune and a new gym floor takes years. A sensor on the weight stack turns the machine a gym already owns into one that counts, in minutes.

new, longevity

Strong for life

76of 100

To copy: Neko Health, its website, member results and voice

Lift for later.

Most people train for the summer. The best reason to lift is the rest of your life: carrying the shopping, getting up off the floor, keeping up with the people you love, at 30 and at 80. The sensor keeps the long record, so every session adds up to that.

See the website, built by copying Neko Health ›

Neko Health says, we say

A record of your bodyA record of your strength

What the research says
30 to 60 minutes of strength training a week is linked to a 10 to 20% lower risk of death (16 studies, British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2022). In 140,000 people in 17 countries, grip strength predicted death better than blood pressure (The Lancet, 2015).
Who it is for
Everyone who thinks about the years ahead, at any age, and the doctors and physios they listen to.
The way in
Real members of every age who lift, filmed in natural light. Physios and doctors who recommend strength. A gentle twelve-week start.
The look
Neko’s look: white, pale blue-grey and beige, a quiet serif over a plain sans, warm black-and-white photos of real people.
What the app must do
It measures years, not workouts: the strength you keep, weeks trained per year, and 30 to 60 minutes of strength a week as the goal.
Why it could win
Longevity is the biggest health conversation right now and strength is its best-proven habit. Older members are also the most loyal ones a gym has.
The risk
Longevity is full of hype and supplements. We promise only what the studies say, with words like "linked to", never "adds years".

Reference brands

  • Neko HealthThe model to copy: calm, precise health checks for everyone, a clinic you look forward to.
  • Function HealthLongevity without the lab coat: soft colours and real people.
  • TimelineScience-backed longevity, said simply.
  • Don't DieThe loud edge of longevity, proof the topic can provoke too. Borrow carefully.

To copy: Brick, its website, app and voice

You lift. The gym writes it down.

A workout today is half lifting and half typing: find the exercise, enter 10, enter 80 kg, start the timer. The sensor does all of it from the machine. You lift, rest and lift again, and the phone stays in the bag.

What the research says
In a Hiram College study, texting during exercise cut balance by 45% and lowered workout intensity.
Who it is for
People tired of screens, and lifters who hate logging apps.
The way in
A "one workout, phone in the locker" challenge. Phone-free hours at partner gyms. The Offline Club crowd.
The look
Brick’s look: forest green, warm grey and one bright blue, a plain Swiss sans, big photos of the object and the tap.
What the app must do
Opened twice, before (the plan) and after (the summary), nothing in between. The rest timer has to reach you without the phone, as a light or sound on the sensor or a buzz on a watch.
Why it could win
It rides a real movement away from screens, with a promise the sensor keeps literally.
The risk
Only true if the rest timer works without the phone. Without that, the promise reads as made up.

Every machine explains itself.

About half of people say the gym intimidates them. Nobody tells you how heavy, how many, or whether you did it right. The sensor on each machine does: it counts, times the rest and says how the set went.

To copy: Ultrahuman, its website, app and voice, already done

Your training, measured.

Watches measure the heart, not the machine, so most strength training has never been measured. Readout puts a sensor on each machine and turns every set into a score anyone can read. It speaks like a lab you can trust.

See the website, built by copying Ultrahuman ›

Who it is for
Data-minded lifters, coaches and serious gyms.
The way in
Building in public from a real gym, the open lab (every count checked by hand), gym myths measured, the week in numbers.
The look
Ultrahuman's calm: off-white, Geist, mono labels, the wave, one blue.
What the app must do
Excellent at the numbers: a score that is right, its contributors, trends. There is nothing else to hide behind.
Why it could win
The most credible voice for serious gyms and for investors.
The risk
A purely professional voice rarely gets shared, so attention has to be bought or earned slowly.

Reference brands

  • UltrahumanScores with contributors, and a lab you can visit.
  • WhoopData as identity, athletes as proof.
  • OuraCalm, human words around hard data.
