How the readout sensor works

The sensor reads reps, sets and rest straight from the weight stack. The app turns them into a score, your progress and one next step, in words anyone can read.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need my phone during a set?

No. The sensor counts on its own. Look at the app before the workout for the plan and after it for the numbers.

Who pays for it?

The gym. Members use the sensor and the app at gyms that run readout.

Which machines does it fit?

Machines with a weight stack come first: leg press, chest press, lat pulldown and the like. Free weights come later.

Does the sensor count right?

That is the test we are running now: a thousand reps, filmed and counted by people. The results go up under How we test, misses included.

What happens to my data?

Your workouts are yours. Gyms see their floor as totals, never one member's sets.

When can my gym get it?

Pilots start with our first gym. Other gyms can ask for a four-week pilot.

Our story

Built on a gym floor.

Watch any gym for an hour and you see the same thing: people working hard and guessing.

How many reps was that? Was the rest two minutes or four? Is this more than last month? The answers live in notebooks, in apps that need typing between sets, or nowhere.

readout started on a gym floor in Brno. We wanted the machines to keep count, so the people on them could keep their heads in the lift. That became a small sensor that clips onto the weight stack and counts every rep, set and rest by itself, and an app that turns those numbers into one plain next step.

We test it in the open. For ninety days our first gym runs on it, and the numbers go public: what works, what we got wrong, what the sensor missed.

What we promise

  • Your workouts are yours. Gyms see their floor as totals, never one member’s sets.
  • Every number comes with a sentence that says what it means.
  • When the sensor gets it wrong, we say so.
